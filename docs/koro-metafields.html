1. About the game → custom.game_description

KORO is a simple puzzle/logic game for Game Boy Color. Manage marbles on a 9x9 grid. Put five or more (same color) in a row to clean a line. Any other move will bring three new marbles (random color, position). Clean as many lines as possible until the board is full and no more moves possible.

No timers, no lives, no hurry — just you, eighty-one squares, and a board that never empties on its own. It is the third iteration of a lineage born in Moscow in 1992 — this is its history.

2. Micro manual → custom.game_manual

DRAFT — review against the printed manual before publishing.

Goal

Clear as many lines as you can. The game ends when the board is full and no move can clear a line.

Rules

  • Marbles sit on a 9×9 grid. Move one marble per turn.
  • Five or more marbles of the same color in a row are cleared from the board.
  • Every move that doesn't clear a line adds three new marbles in random spots.
  • Clear 24 lines to earn the title of Koro Master.

Controls

  • D-pad — move the cursor
  • A — pick up / drop a marble
  • B — cancel a pick-up

3. History & references → custom.game_history

History

1992 — Цветные линии (Color Lines)

The initial Russian version, named Цветные линии (Color Lines). Colloquially known in Russian as Шарики ("Shariki", i.e. "Balls"). Made by Oleg Demin in 1992 for MS-DOS.

[screenshot]

The game was published by Gamos Ltd, a Russian software company, and Wikipedia credits it to a team — Oleg Demin alongside Gennady Denisov and Igor Ivkin. It established the rules every version since has copied: three randomly colored balls per turn on a 9x9 board, five-in-a-row clears, survive as long as you can. It became the template for an entire family of "Lines" games by different authors — the ancestor of everything below.

1995 — Kulki

Second iteration was named Kulki (Balls), developed by Jarosław Lewandowski in 1995 for Windows. It became very popular in Poland. Some people play that version to this day.

[screenshot]

And "very popular" is underselling it: in Polish, kulki simply means "balls", and Polish Wikipedia lists Lewandowski as producer and publisher of this direct variant of Demin's game — same rules, now in a small Windows window. It was released as freeware, and one Polish software archive alone still counts around 290,000 downloads of its roughly 191 kB executable.

2026 — KORO

I found out that there is no modern recreation and nothing even close for the Game Boy. This game is a perfect match for a handheld. So I made the third iteration for it KORO in 2026.

[screenshot]

Why such a perfect match? The rules were practically designed for a handheld from day one. The game is fully turn-based — nothing to time, nothing to react to — so you can drop it into your pocket mid-move and finish it on the bus tomorrow. A 9x9 board sits naturally on the Game Boy Color's 160x144 screen, and the whole game asks for exactly what a handheld offers: a D-pad, two buttons, and time to kill.

Thirty-four years after a Moscow DOS program started it all, the lineage now fits into 138 grams of hardware that runs ten hours.

The hardware it was made for

KORO is dedicated to the Game Boy Color, Nintendo's 1998 handheld — launched in Japan on October 21, 1998, in North America on November 18 and in Europe on November 23. The Game Boy family went on to sell 118.69 million units.
